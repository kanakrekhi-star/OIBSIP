==================================================
1. BASIC SCAN
==================================================

Command:
nmap -Pn 192.168.0.103

Result:

Nmap scan report for 192.168.0.103
Host is up.
All 1000 scanned ports on 192.168.0.103 are in ignored states.
Not shown: 1000 filtered tcp ports (no-response)

Nmap result:
- Host was detected as up.
- 1000 TCP ports were scanned.
- 1000 ports were reported as filtered.
- No open TCP ports were identified by this scan.

Interpretation:
The target computer was reachable, but Nmap did not
receive responses from the scanned TCP ports. The ports
were therefore classified as filtered. This can happen
when firewall or network filtering prevents Nmap from
determining whether the ports are open or closed.

==================================================
2. SERVICE VERSION SCAN
==================================================

Command:
nmap -Pn -sV 192.168.0.103

Result:

Nmap scan report for 192.168.0.103
Host is up.
All 1000 scanned ports on 192.168.0.103 are in ignored states.
Not shown: 1000 filtered tcp ports (no-response)

Service detection performed.

Nmap result:
- Host was detected as up.
- 1000 TCP ports were scanned.
- 1000 ports were filtered.
- No service or version could be identified.

Interpretation:
Because all scanned ports were filtered, Nmap could not
connect to a service and therefore could not determine
the service name or version.

==================================================
3. OS DETECTION SCAN
==================================================

Command:
sudo nmap -Pn -O 192.168.0.103

Result:

Nmap scan report for 192.168.0.103
Host is up.
All 1000 scanned ports on 192.168.0.103 are in ignored states.
Not shown: 1000 filtered tcp ports (no-response)

Warning:
OSScan results may be unreliable because Nmap could not
find at least one open and one closed port.

Nmap generated several aggressive OS guesses, including
Microsoft Windows and Linux systems.

No exact OS match was found.

Nmap result:
- Host was detected as up.
- 1000 TCP ports were filtered.
- OS detection was inconclusive.
- Nmap explicitly stated that the OS results may be unreliable.

Interpretation:
OS detection normally works better when Nmap can examine
the behavior of both open and closed ports. Since all
scanned ports were filtered, the required conditions were
not available and Nmap could not confidently identify
the operating system.

==================================================
4. OPEN PORT AND SERVICE ANALYSIS
==================================================

Confirmed open ports from the required Kali terminal scans:

None.

The required terminal scans did not confirm any open TCP
ports because all 1000 scanned ports were classified as
filtered.

Therefore, no service can be confidently assigned to an
open port from these scans.

==================================================
5. SECURITY ANALYSIS
==================================================

Finding:
All 1000 scanned TCP ports were filtered.

Security impact:
This indicates that network filtering or firewall rules
are preventing Nmap from receiving responses from the
scanned ports. This can reduce exposure to unsolicited
network connections and makes service enumeration more
difficult.

However, filtered ports should not automatically be
considered completely secure. A service could potentially
exist behind firewall rules or be accessible through
another network interface, port, or protocol.

Finding:
Service version detection did not identify any service.

Security impact:
No service version information was exposed during the
scan, which limits the amount of information available to
a scanner.

Finding:
OS detection was inconclusive.

Security impact:
The filtering prevented Nmap from collecting enough
network behavior to make a reliable OS identification.

7. CONCLUSION
==================================================

The Nmap assessment successfully confirmed that the target
Windows computer was online and reachable.

The required scans showed that all 1000 tested TCP ports
were filtered. As a result, no open TCP ports or service

The results demonstrate the importance of firewall and
network filtering in reducing information exposure during
network reconnaissance.
